# Distinct-prime product uniqueness — candidate, not CI verified

Let `ps = [p₀,…,pₘ₋₁]` consist of pairwise distinct primes and let `es` and `fs` be natural-number lists of length `m`. If `∏ pᵢ^esᵢ = ∏ pᵢ^fsᵢ`, then `es = fs`. No injectivity assumption is made. Exponents may be zero. The empty list is included. Prime bases are nonzero, hence both products are nonzero. By uniqueness of prime factorization, the exponent of `pᵢ` in either product is exactly its corresponding list entry; distinctness ensures every other base contributes zero. Evaluating the equal products' factorizations at each `pᵢ` gives equality entry by entry. The Lean candidate instead cancels the equal first powers and inducts down the list; the Mathlib ingredients are `Nat.factorization_mul` and `Nat.Prime.factorization_pow`.

The occurrence bridge still requires a proof that the aligned base list is `p (j-1), p (j-2), …` for exactly the length of the choices, and that its list product equals `RenewalOccurrenceIndex.pathFactor p j choices`. In particular, `pathFactor` ignores choices after level zero, so length/shape assumptions are essential. Then combine product uniqueness with the existing `path_encoding_unique_position`; no position or factor bijection is claimed here.

Counterexamples: bases `[2,2]`, exponents `[1,0]` and `[0,1]` both give 2; bases `[2,4]`, exponents `[2,0]` and `[0,1]` both give 4. Thus repeated prime bases or distinct composite bases can each invalidate uniqueness.
